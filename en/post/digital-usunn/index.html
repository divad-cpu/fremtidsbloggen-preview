Digitally Unhealthy

Unhealthy products. Objects that are not healthy. These could be skin-care products or something technical in mobile devices, such as Bluetooth technology.

Do we know with 100% certainty that this wave of digital interference is good for all of us?

To be 100% accurate about which products contain suspicious 'ingredients' or a new technological feature, they must go through a system of 100% accurate and correct testing that meets sustainability and health measures.

Measures that take every possible way into account.

We use electronics and wireless systems as never before. We feel safe with devices on and around us. We trust researchers to examine our digital lifestyle fully so that they can assure us that everything is in order.

But can we trust researchers to be 100% accurate about what is healthy or not? No, not yet. We are too early in our time with the technologies of our generations to know exactly, with 100% certainty, what is healthy or not.

We continue to use everything until it is discovered that something is not good for our health.

We will continue to invent unhealthy technology until we discover what it really does to us.

Look back in time at earlier days when lead was used in various products — lead in toothpaste?! — until it was discovered to be unhealthy. This could happen with products we use today, depending on what we know.

Products made today, whether digital, technical or ordinary household products, undergo quality testing and assurance, and there should be a reliable way to test whether something is unhealthy.

Creating good enough procedures for every product is an almost endless task, with one exception: through humanity's strong will, we will invent and find solutions to ensure a product's accuracy, allowing us to measure against a 'standard' based on different levels of unhealthiness on a scale that only improves with the help of technology itself. A small paradox: technology finds out how unhealthy technology is on a scale from 1.01 to 100.001.
