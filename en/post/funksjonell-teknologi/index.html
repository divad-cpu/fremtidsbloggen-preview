Developing functional and user-friendly technology.

Technology-savvy people will accept and use tomorrow's technology in their everyday lives year after year.

Embracing technological functionality one can relate to gives it a natural meaning.

If technology can become part of our habits, like something as natural as checking our smartphones using the internet.

Restaurants and nightlife will soon embrace technologies in their shared environments. Some in these industries already do this, but more will follow.

When ordering food and drink from the table becomes something out of the ordinary, we can begin to talk about functional development in this ordinary industry.

The iPhone has become a functional and user-friendly technical device. Most people immediately know how to use it.

When a technical device is introduced to the market, a common sense of preference is based on how well it will work with my habits. Can the device be used exactly as I expect? Can it be used as often and be as useful as an iPhone?

Developing a device or program to meet most people's pervasive standards is no easy task. Looking at collective awareness is the key to doing this.

Including well-integrated technology should be regarded as important both for most individuals and for those developing these innovative and central ideas.

To understand a person's pervasive rational standard, there must be a sense of fairness and empathy for the customer you serve.

You must understand how they feel in front of the technology that was or is being used at the time, and look back by asking how it was before. Look ahead and ask them what they would add or like.

Pay attention to what functional technology means to the person using your technology; be at the centre of attention.
